# Literature-Informed Design of emerge-mapper's Four-Level Compositional Hierarchy

## TL;DR
- **The working hypothesis is right in spirit but wrong in its strongest form.** The modern shape-synthesis literature (StructureNet, the Chaudhuri–Ritchie survey, USD composition) strongly endorses ONE arbitrary-depth recursive composition type over fixed-arity levels — but it also names the exact failure you risk: representational NON-UNIQUENESS and inconsistency. Unify Assembly and Arrange into one `Composition{Envelope, Policy}`; keep Map a distinct type that HOLDS references (I1, I9).
- **Derived interfaces, reference-by-default with reasoned overrides, and truthful staleness are all directly supported** by strong sources — Sturgeon's tag indirection (I2/I3), USD's LIVRPS + encapsulation (I4), and Build Systems à la Carte's verifying traces + early cutoff (I5) — and each maps cleanly onto commitments you already hold ("reasons, not booleans"; the rig watcher).
- **Two connection models (sockets vs edge tokens) should stay two types**, not one: the literature models them as genuinely different (anti-parallel attachment-point matching in Megaro robotic creatures / Gal collage vs equal-token grid abutment in Merrell/WFC/Sturgeon). Keep quasimodal overlays over mode toggles (Raskin), and enforce single-source-of-truth counters (I10, I12).

## Key Findings

1. **One recursive composition type is the modern consensus, with a named risk.** GRASS (Li, Xu, Chaudhuri, Yumer, Zhang, Guibas, SIGGRAPH 2017) used fixed binary recursion; StructureNet (Mo, Guerrero et al., SIGGRAPH Asia 2019) explicitly abandoned fixed arity for arbitrary-depth n-ary hierarchical graphs, because "the required binary symmetry hierarchy can introduce arbitrary ordering in nodes and make the hierarchy inconsistent" — its central technical novelty is an "order-invariant encoding of n-ary graphs." This validates a single parameterised `Composition` but warns that the danger of over-unification is ambiguity/non-uniqueness, not "levels."
2. **Derived interfaces are correct-by-construction but fragile to member drift.** Sturgeon's tag indirection and Bass's integrability chapter both support computing an interface from members; Bass warns the real cost is the "distance" (syntactic/semantic/temporal/resource) that silently changes when a member changes.
3. **USD is the closest existing system to your reference/override/break-link model** and supplies both a strength-ordering discipline (LIVRPS) and a hard lesson (encapsulation: the internal structure of a referenced arc is immutable from outside, which is precisely what makes composition "properly recursive").
4. **Truthful staleness four levels deep is a solved problem** if you adopt verifying traces + early cutoff (Build Systems à la Carte, DOI 10.1145/3236774) — which your rig watcher already approximates.
5. **Scoped generation composes only if the outer boundary condition is injected as a constraint at the inner scope** — Sturgeon's "linking level segments" and Heese's Hierarchical WFC (HWFC) partitioning are the direct precedents.

## Details

### I1. One type vs many

**Sources:** GRASS (Li, Xu, Chaudhuri, Yumer, Zhang, Guibas, *ACM TOG* 36(4):52, 2017, DOI 10.1145/3072959.3073613, arXiv 1705.02090); StructureNet (Mo, Guerrero, Yi, Su, Wonka, Mitra, Guibas, *ACM TOG* 38(6):242, 2019, DOI 10.1145/3355089.3356527, arXiv 1908.00575); "Learning Generative Models of 3D Structures" (Chaudhuri, Ritchie, Wu, Xu, Zhang, *Computer Graphics Forum* 39(2):643–666, 2020, DOI 10.1111/cgf.14020); PartField (Liu, Uy, Xiang, Su, Fidler, Sharp, Gao, 2025, DOI 10.48550/arXiv.2504.11451); Chaudhuri, Kalogerakis, Guibas & Koltun (2011, DOI 10.1145/1964921.1964930, stem 10.1145_1964921.1964930).

**What they say.** The modern representation is arbitrary-depth recursive composition where "each node can itself expand into another hierarchical graph, terminating at nodes which represent atomic geometry" (Chaudhuri et al. survey). GRASS imposed a fixed binary merge; StructureNet explicitly rejected that, because the binarisation "can introduce arbitrary ordering in nodes and make the hierarchy inconsistent," and its fix is to design "the encoder and the decoder to be invariant of the ordering of siblings across instances of the n-ary graphs." Chaudhuri et al. 2011 already decomposes hierarchically (torso → upper/lower torso). PartField builds a hierarchical tree of parts by agglomerative clustering over face adjacency and lets users "adaptively select the branches that require further decomposition" — arbitrary-depth drill-down in a shipped interactive tool.

**How it applies.** This is direct, strong support for collapsing Assembly and Arrange into ONE `Composition` type parameterised by `Envelope` (Bounded-to-tile vs Anchored-unbounded) and `Policy`, giving recursive containment (Meal in Table Setting in Dining Room) for free with no "which level contains which" rule — exactly the survey's "node expands into another hierarchical graph."

**Where it contradicts the hypothesis.** The literature's cited risk is precisely over-unification into a single uniform recursive merge: it produces representational non-uniqueness (the survey: "there are many possible hierarchies for each graph, depending on the order of edge contraction chosen"; StructureNet: the binary constraint "make[s] the hierarchy inconsistent"). For you this surfaces as: the SAME set of members could be encoded as different composition trees, so two authors (or the same author twice) produce diffs that differ without meaning differing — a violation of your "reasons, not booleans" ethos at the structural level. **Recommendation:** unify Assembly+Arrange, but make the containment tree canonical and explicit (author-declared parenthood with a reason on every non-obvious grouping, and order-invariant sibling encoding à la StructureNet), not inferred by an ordering heuristic. Do NOT fold Map into the same type (see I9).

### I2. Derived vs authored interfaces

**Sources:** Sturgeon (Cooper, *AIIDE* 2022, DOI 10.1609/aiide.v18i1.21944); Bass, Clements & Kazman, *Software Architecture in Practice* 4th ed. (stem bass_software_architecture_in_practice_4th), integrability chapter; Megaro et al. robotic creatures (stem eth-cgl-materials-Meg15a); StructureNet (relation edges).

**What they say.** Sturgeon separates a FUNCTIONAL grid from an IMAGE grid and uses a TAG indirection layer: tags "limit what tiles can be placed at a location," and "functional tiles can be used as tags to constrain image tile placement." The interface (what may abut) is thus derived from a lower layer, not hand-authored per tile. Megaro's structural features each "output a set of possible attachment points," and connection geometry is synthesised between mapped attachment-point sets — the interface is computed from the members. Bass's integrability chapter insists interfaces "must be understood as much more than simply APIs" and that the hard part is the "distance" between elements — syntactic, data-semantic, behavioral-semantic, temporal, resource — much of which is implicit and only surfaces at integration.

**How it applies.** Strong support for: an Assembly's edge tokens are READ OFF the boundary subgrid cells of its members (nobody hand-writes them); an Arrangement's exposed sockets are the union of member sockets no sibling consumed. This is "making illegal states unrepresentable" — you cannot author an inconsistent interface because there is no authoring surface for it.

**Known failure mode.** Bass's "distance" concept predicts the exact break: when a member changes, the derived interface changes SILENTLY and every parent's contract shifts underneath it. This is an accidental interface change. **Recommendation:** keep interfaces derived (correct-by-construction), but pair every derived interface with a recorded fingerprint of the members it was derived from (see I5), so that a member change makes dependents STALE rather than silently-wrong. Author overrides must carry a reason (consistent with `Placed::owned_because`) — the override is the one place authored data re-enters, and it must name why the derived value was insufficient.

### I3. Two connection models

**Sources:** Megaro et al. (eth-cgl-materials-Meg15a) and the linkage-character companion (eth-cgl-materials-Meg14a, eth-cgl-materials-Tho14a); Gal et al. "3D Collage" (stem 3D-Collage-Expressive-Non-Realistic-Modeling); Merrell model synthesis (stem merrell09; and *IEEE TVCG* 2010, DOI 10.1109/tvcg.2010.112); Graph-based WFC (Kim, Hahn, Kim & Kang, *IEICE Trans. Inf. Syst.* 2020, DOI 10.1587/transinf.2019edp7295); Sturgeon (DOI 10.1609/aiide.v18i1.21944); Boris the Brave, "Constraint-Based Tile Generators" (boristhebrave.com).

**What they say.** Attachment-point models (Megaro, Gal collage) match by GEOMETRY — position + orientation, with connection geometry synthesised between paired sets; the match is fundamentally directional/anti-parallel (two faces pointing at each other). Edge/label models (Merrell, WFC, Sturgeon, Wang tiles) match by EQUAL TOKENS across a shared grid face; Boris the Brave classifies these as the "adjacency" model (labels must match, aka Wang tiles) distinct from the "content-based" model (mesh boundary geometry). Graph-based WFC generalises adjacency off the square grid onto a connection graph — the propagator drops direction and works purely on connectivity, using a dictionary rather than a fixed 3D array because a node's neighbour count is "limitless."

**How it applies.** Sockets (directed, anti-parallel facing match, kind + accepted-token set + optional required-with-reason) map onto the attachment-point family; edge tokens (equal tokens across a shared grid face, consumed by adjacency reporter and WFC) map onto the Wang-tile/label family. These are genuinely different matching predicates in the literature: anti-parallel geometric alignment vs symbolic equality on a shared face.

**Verdict against unification.** Do NOT unify them into one type. Graph-based WFC shows you CAN put both on a common graph substrate at the solver level (nodes + typed edges), but the MATCHING SEMANTICS differ (equality vs anti-parallel geometry) and collapsing them would force one predicate to masquerade as the other — the classic over-unification error. **Recommendation:** keep Socket and EdgeToken as two types with two match predicates; if you want a shared abstraction, share the graph/propagator substrate (à la graph-based WFC) not the match rule. This also respects "one path per feature": each connection kind has exactly one matcher.

### I4. Reference vs flatten, overrides, break-link

**Sources:** OpenUSD composition arcs / LIVRPS (openusd.org FAQ, NVIDIA Learn OpenUSD, aousd.org); Unity nested prefabs / prefab variants (docs.unity3d.com; bugnet.io failure analyses); Unreal Level Instances vs Packed Level Actors (dev.epicgames.com); Media Molecule Dreams clone (gamedeveloper.com).

**What they say.** USD composes by REFERENCE with sparse overrides ("overs") and a canonical strength order LIVRPS — confirmed verbatim as "local, inherits, variant sets, references, payloads, and specializes" from strongest to weakest, and recently extended to LIVERPS where the added "E" is "rElocates." Critically, USD enforces ENCAPSULATION: "whenever you use a references, payload, inherits, variantSet, or specializes arc on a prim, the result that gets woven into the composition is immutable by stronger layers — values can be overridden, and objects can be added, but the 'internal referencing structure' of the arc's target cannot be changed," and this is exactly what "makes the LIVRPS composition algorithm explainable … because it is properly recursive." Unity's nested-prefab/variant system is the cautionary tale: "Apply All walks the chain from the instance outward, applying each override to the appropriate layer — but appropriate is Unity's best guess," and overrides "evaporate when someone applies changes upward, when a nested prefab loses the object the override targets, or when … stored file IDs go out of sync." Unreal distinguishes Level Instances (editable references, any actor types) from Packed Level Actors (flattened, static-mesh-only, batched to ISM/HISM for draw-call efficiency) — a reference-vs-flatten split made on a performance axis. Dreams' clone is a live instance where edits propagate, showing propagation-by-default is a shippable consumer-grade model.

**How it applies.** Reference-by-default with an explicit override list and break-link as a named verb is well-supported and closest to USD. Adopt LIVRPS's discipline: a fixed, documented strength order for where an opinion wins (member default < derived interface < author override), so override resolution is deterministic and explainable — this is your "single key census with a collision test" applied to override precedence.

**Where override systems become unpredictable.** Unity names it exactly: overrides break when the target they point at moves or disappears, and when bulk-apply guesses the wrong layer. **Recommendations:** (1) enforce USD-style encapsulation — a child's internal structure is immutable from the parent; the parent may override leaf values and add, not restructure. (2) Make overrides target STABLE identity (your single key census), never positional/ordinal identity, so a member reorder cannot silently reassign an override (the Unity file-ID failure). (3) break-link is a deliberate verb that BAKES current overrides as owned data and severs propagation — mirror Unreal's "convert Packed Level Actor" and Unity's "unpack" (overrides get "baked onto the resulting GameObjects … no longer have status as overrides"). Never offer an implicit or automatic break.

### I5. Staleness and invalidation across four levels

**Sources:** Build Systems à la Carte (Mokhov, Mitchell & Peyton Jones, *Proc. ACM PL* 2(ICFP):79, 2018, DOI 10.1145/3236774; journal version *JFP* 30:e11, 2020); Bevy asset system (docs.rs/bevy; DeepWiki bevy_asset dependency tracking; bevyengine/bevy issues #3759, #18358); your existing rig watcher.

**What they say.** Build Systems à la Carte factors any correct incremental system into a scheduler + a rebuilder, and identifies EARLY CUTOFF as the key optimisation: when a task re-runs and its result is unchanged, dependents need not rebuild. It distinguishes VERIFYING traces — verbatim, "an alternative way to determine if a key is dirty is to record the values/hashes of dependencies used last time, and if something has changed, the key is dirty and must be rebuilt" — from CONSTRUCTIVE traces (store the value itself, enabling cloud sharing), and warns that "a downside of deep constructive traces is that they cannot support early cutoff, other than at n levels of dependencies." Make approximates this with mtimes but cannot get early cutoff right because it can only "update to the latest modification time." Bevy's asset system reference-counts handles and "automatically tracks nested assets and recursive load states," but its Scene hot-reload is historically incomplete (only DynamicScenes reload cleanly; multi-mesh glTF scene reloads duplicate or partially replace entities) — a caution that engine-level dependency tracking is not a free lunch.

**How it applies.** Your rig watcher — count an mtime change only when seen equal on two consecutive polls (never measure a half-written export), plus a persisted cache entry that survives only if the asset still exists, the manifest record equals the one measured under, and the bytes hash to a recorded fingerprint — is already a VERIFYING TRACE with a debounce. Generalise it verbatim up all four levels: each Composition stores, per member, the fingerprint of the member interface it was built against. A Describe-level edit changes a descriptor's fingerprint → dependent Assemblies see a mismatch → STALE; if the recomputed Assembly interface hash is UNCHANGED, early cutoff stops propagation to Arrangements and Maps.

**How to keep STALE truthful without over-invalidating.** Early cutoff is the whole answer: invalidate on fingerprint MISMATCH, not on any edit; stop propagating the instant a recomputed interface hashes equal to the recorded one. Use verifying (hash) traces, not deep constructive traces, precisely because the latter forfeit early cutoff. **Recommendation:** one fingerprint per derived interface (I2), checked against the recorded fingerprint on the parent's dependency edge; the two-consecutive-equal-polls debounce protects against mid-write reads at every level. Do not rely on Bevy's Scene hot-reload for correctness — treat the engine as the spawner/blender (your "borrowed, never copied") but own the dependency graph in emerge-core.

### I6. Scoped / hierarchical generation

**Sources:** Heese "Quantum Wave Function Collapse" incl. Hierarchical WFC / HWFC (*IEEE CG&A* 2024, DOI 10.1109/mcg.2024.3447775); Sturgeon level infilling / linking / repair (DOI 10.1609/aiide.v18i1.21944); Merrell large-scale constraints (merrell09; DOI 10.1109/tvcg.2010.112); Graph-based WFC (DOI 10.1587/transinf.2019edp7295); Boris the Brave (BorderConstraint, path constraint, "pre-author content and let WFC draw around it").

**What they say.** Heese's HWFC partitions the output into H equal-sized partitions solved hierarchically (e.g. each partition a half-row of a 10×10 level, with H=20). Sturgeon's "infill" uses hard constraints on output tiles with an empty interior area to be filled — "could be used in co-creative tools where a designer could request suggestions for specific areas" — and "link" joins two fixed segments through a constrained gap. Merrell's large-scale constraints are "specified on a large volumetric grid where each voxel records which objects should appear within it" — an outer scope dictating inner content. Boris the Brave's BorderConstraint fixes the boundary tiles so generation "doesn't lead off the edge," and pre-authored tiles "get seamlessly integrated into the generation."

**How it applies.** Running the `G`-key grammar-learned WFC at a NEW SCOPE (inside an Assembly's tile envelope, solving for pieces satisfying the tile's declared edge tokens) is exactly Sturgeon-infill / Boris border-constraint: the outer scope's declared edge tokens become FIXED boundary constraints on the inner solve. Adjacency constraints DO compose across scopes provided the boundary condition is injected as a hard constraint at the inner scope — this is the whole point of HWFC partitioning and Sturgeon linking.

**What changes at the inner scope.** The inner constraint set must be consistent with the outer boundary, which can make the inner problem UNSAT even when it would be satisfiable in isolation (Boris: "with multiple constraints it can be hard for the WFC to find a valid solution; turning on backtracking can alleviate this"). **Recommendation:** inject the tile envelope's declared edge tokens as fixed border constraints on the inner WFC; on inner-scope contradiction, refuse and name the conflicting boundary token (your "refuses rather than opening empty"), rather than emitting a degraded fill (your no-fallbacks rule). WFC/model-synthesis is NP-hard and can always contradict, so a refusal path is mandatory anyway.

### I7. Mixed-initiative authoring at multiple levels

**Sources:** PCG book ch. 11 (stems pcgbook-ch11-mixed-initiative-content-creation, pcgbook_chapter11) — Tanagra, Sentient Sketchbook, Ropossum; Liapis, Yannakakis & Alexopoulos "Mixed-Initiative Co-Creativity" (*FDG* 2014, stem fdg2014_fdg2014_paper_37); Evolutionary Dungeon Designer (DOI 10.1145/3235765.3235815); Planner-Actor-Critic (Gao & Juluri, 2026, DOI 10.48550/arXiv.2601.05016).

**What they say.** Carbonell's founding MI principles are CONTEXT and RELEVANCY: the computer should only make contextually relevant suggestions, and — verbatim from ch.11 — "in general, the computer is never allowed to override a decision made by the human." Liapis et al. warn evaluation must capture the "proactive computational initiative … on human creativity," and value re-asking a generator (an "unusual but legal move" can be more creative). The Planner-Actor-Critic paper reports the concrete degradation you'll hit: "after multiple iterations (often three), the modeling quality degrades or converge[s], making further improvements difficult," and "the actor does not always incorporate the critic's feedback" — plus human intervention "helps the system escape local optima."

**How it applies.** Running the human/computer loop at several hierarchy levels at once (Describe classification suggestions, Assembly WFC fill, Arrange socket-snap, Map generate) risks the designer losing their model of WHICH scope the system is acting in — Carbonell's "context" is exactly the thing that degrades when initiative is shared across nested scopes. **Recommendation:** scope the initiative loop to exactly one level at a time and make the active scope unambiguous (ties into I10's read-only overlay and I11's ghost). Preserve "re-ask for a different answer" as a first-class verb (Liapis; also your existing WFC re-roll). Keep the human's decisions authoritative and never let a higher-scope generate silently rewrite a lower-scope authored choice — this is Carbonell's non-override principle and your "reasons, not booleans."

### I8. Migration of existing content

**Sources:** Bass 4th ed. integrability (intermediaries/adapters vs standardisation); Begemann & Hutson "Prompted Props, Human Pipelines" (2026, DOI 10.55677/ijhrsss/15-2026-vol03i06); general schema-evolution practice.

**What they say.** Bass frames adapters/intermediaries as a way to "break dependencies" and bridge distance — but notes each intermediary adds a component whose behaviour must itself be understood and maintained ("an analyst needs to determine whether the intermediary reduces the number of dependencies … and which dimensions of distance, if any, it addresses"). Begemann & Hutson argue the decisive move is a GATE at the moment content enters the repository ("gatekeeping procedures at the moment AI-generated assets enter a production repository"), because "speed without asset-readiness can conceal technical debt."

**How it applies.** Auto-promoting each of your 120 flat descriptors into a single-member Assembly preserving id (one-shot) versus a lazy compatibility shim: the shim is exactly an intermediary/adapter, and Bass's warning is that it becomes a permanent second code path whose distance must be maintained — which directly violates your "one path per feature; no fallbacks." Begemann & Hutson's gate argues for converting at the boundary once. **Recommendation:** do the one-shot migration. It preserves a single schema and a single path; the shim would institutionalise a second, degraded path forever. The literature here is thin (no rigorous head-to-head on one-shot vs adapter for authored game content) — this is largely an engineering-judgement call, but both the architecture principle (avoid a permanent adapter) and your own no-fallbacks commitment point the same way.

### I9. Unifying Map or keeping it distinct

**Sources:** StructureNet / Chaudhuri–Ritchie survey (I1); USD encapsulation and stage model (I4); Unreal Level Instance vs Packed Level Actor (I4); Bass modifiability/reversibility.

**What they say.** The survey treats Map-like arrangement as just another level of the hierarchical graph — arguing FOR unification. But USD keeps a distinct top-level "stage" that COMPOSES referenced layers rather than being one; Unreal keeps the Level as a distinct container that HOLDS Level Instances. Both shipped systems keep the top container distinct while making its CONTENTS recursive.

**How it applies and the reversibility argument.** Map already ships as a tested schema (`emerge_core::map::Map`) with bounds + stack/layer system, and the same schema/solvers run in game, headless search, and editor. Two options: (a) Map = `Composition{Envelope::Bounded, + stack}`; (b) Map stays a distinct type that merely holds references to Compositions. The reversibility asymmetry is decisive: keeping Map distinct now but letting it hold references is EASILY promoted to full unification later (add an Envelope field); but unifying now and discovering Map's stack/layer/bounds semantics don't fit the recursive `Composition` cleanly is a shipped-schema migration under your "every load failure is fatal" regime. **Recommendation:** keep Map a distinct type that holds references to Compositions (option b). It matches how USD (stage) and Unreal (level) actually ship, protects the tested schema, and preserves the cheaper-to-reverse decision. Revisit unification only if a concrete need for Map-inside-Map appears.

### I10. Input scheme and mode proliferation

**Sources:** Raskin, *The Humane Interface* (modes vs quasimodes, via raskincenter.org and secondary summaries); mode-error literature.

**What they say.** Raskin: modes cause errors "because humans simply forget which mode they're in"; his solution is the QUASIMODE — verbatim, "a state that can only be maintained through continuous user action. The shift key is the canonical quasimode." "Caps Lock, by contrast, is a true mode … simply bad design. The quasimode alternative — a key that must be held — eliminates the error pattern." Experiments confirm that physically holding an interface in a state does not induce mode errors.

**How it applies.** A single `Compose` key context shared by Assembly/Arrange/Map (rather than three near-identical contexts that drift) is well-founded — fewer modes, one path. The friction is Assembly, where Describe's cell-walking/classification bindings collide with Compose's placement bindings on ~5 keys. Your proposed READ-ONLY cell overlay rather than a mode toggle is exactly Raskin's prescription: an overlay that shows classification without a persistent mode you can forget. If the overlay needs to become interactive, make it QUASIMODAL (held key) so it cannot be forgotten. **Recommendation:** single `Compose` context; cell classification as a read-only overlay; if interactive cell-walking is needed inside Assembly, bind it to a held (quasimodal) key, never a toggled mode. This also serves I7 by making the active scope self-evident. Resolve the ~5-key collision through your single key census with a collision test (contexts make legal collisions explicit) — the census is itself the mode-proliferation guard.

### I11. Preview fidelity at depth

**Sources:** Feedforward literature (Vermeulen et al. "Interaction Frogger"; Muresan et al. feedforward in VR, *ACM TOCHI* 30(4), 2023, DOI 10.1145/3603623; recent feedforward design-space work, arXiv 2502.14229, 2602.13433); your "the ghost is the contract"; Unreal PLA batching for instanced preview.

**What they say.** Feedforward = "cues that indicate the possible outcomes of an action before it is executed, helping users anticipate system behavior and direct themselves to their goal." The design-space work distinguishes previewing on the original vs DUPLICATING the visual (the "duplication parameter") and previewing OUTCOMES vs ACTIONS. The consistent principle: feedforward must show the RESOLVED outcome, not the raw input.

**How it applies.** "The ghost is the contract" IS feedforward, and the literature sharpens your two hard cases: in Arrange the ghost must show the RESOLVED socket snap (the outcome), not the cursor position (the raw action) — this is precisely "preview the outcome, not the input." In Map, an arrangement's ghost must preview ACTUAL CONTENTS, not a proxy box. The performance-vs-fidelity tension is real: previewing full composite contents at every cursor move is expensive. Unreal's answer (Packed Level Actors batching identical meshes to ISM/HISM) is the pattern — preview real content but batch instanced draws. **Recommendation:** ghost renders the resolved snap and real contents, borrowed through the game's real spawner/blender (your "borrowed, never copied"), with instanced batching for repeated members; if fidelity cannot be met within frame budget, DRAW NO GHOST rather than a proxy box — your own rule ("a preview drawn where the piece will not land is worse than no preview") is the literature's outcome-fidelity principle stated correctly.

### I12. Derived-metric drift

**Sources:** Build Systems à la Carte (single authoritative store, DOI 10.1145/3236774); Bass (single source of truth / information hiding); Sturgeon (functional vs image grid as separated derived views); your own bug (tiles.rs "360 meshes, 41 in library, ~319 candidates"; vlm.rs "131 library entries and ~319 unlabeled candidates"; actual 75 descriptors).

**What they say.** The build-systems and architecture literature is unanimous that derived quantities must be COMPUTED from one authoritative store, never cached in parallel copies — this is the same discipline as early cutoff (recompute from the store; don't trust a stale copy). Bass's information-hiding tactic hides the representation behind one accessor.

**How it applies.** Three different numbers for one quantity is derived-state drift: each site cached its own count. A four-level hierarchy multiplies "N of M described/assembled/arranged" counters. **Recommendation:** compute every count on demand from the single authoritative catalog (the same store the census lives in); never store a count as data. This is your "single key census in one table" extended to metrics — one table, all counters derived, so drift is unrepresentable. Pair with a test that asserts independently-computed counts agree (the collision-test analogue for metrics).

## Recommendations (staged)

**Stage 1 — lock the type model (before any code).**
- Unify Assembly + Arrange into `Composition{Envelope, Policy}`; keep Map a distinct reference-holding type (I1, I9).
- Keep Socket and EdgeToken as two types with two match predicates; optionally share a graph/propagator substrate only (I3).
- Adopt reference-by-default + reasoned overrides + explicit break-link, with USD-style encapsulation and stable-identity override targets (I4).
- *Threshold to revisit:* if a concrete Map-in-Map need appears, reconsider I9.

**Stage 2 — build the invalidation spine.**
- Generalise the rig watcher into a verifying-trace dependency graph: one fingerprint per derived interface, early cutoff on unchanged hashes, two-poll debounce at every level (I2, I5).
- Compute all counters from the single catalog; add the agreement test (I12).

**Stage 3 — generation and interaction.**
- Inject outer edge tokens as fixed border constraints on inner-scope WFC; refuse-and-name on contradiction (I6).
- One `Compose` context; read-only (or quasimodal) cell overlay; resolve key collisions via the census (I10).
- Scope the mixed-initiative loop to one level; keep re-roll first-class; never let a higher scope silently rewrite lower authored choices (I7).
- Ghost shows resolved snap + real batched contents or nothing (I11).

**Stage 4 — migrate.**
- One-shot promote the 120 descriptors to single-member Assemblies preserving id; no shim (I8).
